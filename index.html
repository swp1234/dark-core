About the Dark Core: D-Factor, Traits & FAQ

Psychologists define the Dark Core (the 'D-factor') as the underlying dispositional tendency to prioritize self-interest over collective welfare, often rationalized by beliefs that justify unethical behavior (Moshagen, Hilbig, & Zettler, 2018).

Clinical Boundary: This test explores subclinical personality tendencies for self-awareness and philosophical reflection. It does not diagnose clinical DSM-5 personality disorders.

The Dark Tetrad Dimensions

  • Narcissism: Grandiosity, entitlement, and an unquenchable desire for social validation and superiority.
  • Machiavellianism: Strategic calculating, emotional detachment, and pragmatic manipulation to achieve long-term aims.
  • Psychopathy: Blunted empathy, thrill-seeking impulsivity, and diminished social guilt or anxiety.
  • Sadism: Everyday cruelty and deriving psychological satisfaction from asserting dominance over others.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What is the D-factor and how does it relate to the Dark Triad?

A: The D-factor is the common psychological denominator unifying dark traits: pursuing personal benefit regardless of harm inflicted upon others.

Q: Is this test a psychiatric evaluation?

A: No. It is an educational tool for exploring subclinical moral tendencies and does not constitute psychiatric or clinical screening.

Q: Can someone possess dark traits without acting maliciously?

A: Yes. Many individuals channel traits like high Machiavellianism into strategic leadership or negotiation while maintaining ethical self-discipline.

Q: Are my dilemma choices or scores collected?

A: Never. All calculations are executed completely inside your browser. No moral decisions, profile scores, or analytics are stored externally.
